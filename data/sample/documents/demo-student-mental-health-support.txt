Young people increasingly say that stress, anxiety and loneliness affect their studies and their lives. This briefing from Demo Student Wellbeing Group summarises what students told us about mental health and wellbeing, and what universities and governments should do.

In our survey, most students reported feeling under pressure because of exams, the cost of living and uncertainty about work. Counselling services exist, but waiting times of several weeks are common, and the number of free sessions is limited. Students who live far from home, who work while studying or who have a disability face the biggest barriers. Many do not ask for psychological support because they fear stigma or do not know where to go.

We recommend that every university offers free and confidential counselling, with enough psychologists for the number of students and a maximum waiting time of two weeks. Teachers and administrative staff should receive basic training to recognise signs of distress and refer students to help. Exam calendars should be reviewed to reduce stress, with more flexible sessions and clear rules for students who fall ill.

Prevention matters as much as treatment. Campaigns against stigma, affordable sport and cultural activities, and places where students can meet outside lessons all support wellbeing. Peer support schemes, run by trained students, can reach those who would never book an appointment.

Mental health support is not a luxury. It is a condition for learning, and a public responsibility.
