Young people make up a large share of the European population, yet their voices rarely shape the decisions that affect them. This position paper from Demo Youth Network looks at how youth participation works in practice in European policy making and where it falls short.

Our members report three recurring problems. First, consultations open late, often when the main choices have already been made. Second, the language of consultation documents is technical, which excludes young people who are not already part of policy circles. Third, there is almost never feedback: participants do not learn what happened to their input.

We recommend a standing youth panel attached to each major policy file, with a fixed seat for representatives of youth organisations and a random selection of young citizens. Consultations should run for at least twelve weeks, be published in plain language and be open in all official languages. Every institution that consults young people should publish a short response explaining which proposals were taken up, which were not, and why.

Youth rights are also at stake. Voting at sixteen in European elections, protection from discrimination in internships and access to information about their rights are all questions where young people should be heard first. A youth policy that is made for young people rather than with them will not hold.

Finally, participation needs resources. Travel costs, interpretation and staff time should be covered so that small youth organisations, and not only large ones, can take part. Without this, participation stays a privilege.
