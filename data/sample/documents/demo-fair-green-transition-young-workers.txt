The shift to a low-carbon economy will create millions of jobs, but climate justice means asking who gets them. This briefing from Demo Climate Coalition examines how the green transition affects young workers and what governments should do about it.

Young people carry the longest burden of climate change, and they enter a labour market that is changing fast. Many green jobs are short contracts, internships or platform work with little protection. Others require skills that schools and universities do not yet teach. Without action, the transition will reproduce existing inequalities between regions, between income groups and between generations.

We recommend three measures. First, funded retraining and apprenticeship schemes in regions that depend on fossil fuels, open to workers under thirty and designed with local youth groups. Second, minimum standards for green jobs, including fair pay for traineeships and a right to a written contract. Third, a seat for young people in national energy and climate planning bodies, so that sustainability policy reflects the interests of those who will live with its results.

Environmental policy must also be fair in how its costs are shared. Carbon pricing without social support hits low-income households hardest. Public transport, building renovation grants and affordable renewable energy for young tenants should come before new taxes.

Climate action is popular among young people, but trust is fragile. A transition that is seen to be fair will be accepted and defended; one that is seen as unfair will be rejected.
