EU funding is meant to support civil society in all its diversity, but in practice the biggest grants go to the biggest organisations. This paper from Demo Youth Network explains why small organisations struggle to access European funding and how the system could change.

Applications are long and written in a technical style that often requires a paid consultant. Co-financing rules oblige an organisation to find part of the money elsewhere before it can receive any, and payments arrive late, so small groups must carry the cost for months. Reporting rules ask for detailed proof of every expense, which takes staff time that small teams do not have. Mistakes in these reports can lead to repayment demands that threaten the survival of the organisation.

We propose five reforms. Use lump sums and simplified cost options wherever possible. Offer a short first-stage application so that organisations do not write a full proposal that fails. Pay at least half of the grant in advance. Create a single contact point at each institution that answers questions in plain language. Fund core costs, not only projects, so that organisations can keep their staff between grants.

Governance matters too. Civil society should be part of the design of funding programmes, not only a recipient. Regular dialogue with the European institutions would show early where rules do not work.

A funding system that excludes small organisations loses the local knowledge and trust that make European policy credible.
